# The Threshold That Just Moved

A small example from this morning. I asked Claude to rename five plan files in a side project. The originals were inconsistently named: `NEXT_SESSION_PLAN.md` in screaming snake case, `verify_plan_2.md` in lowercase with a numbered suffix, others in their own conventions. The rename meant changing five filenames, moving them to a different directory, and updating cross-references in three other files (one of which had a half-dozen mentions sprinkled across journal-style entries).

Pre-Claude, I would not have done this. The expected-value math killed it before it started: maybe thirty minutes of focused yak-shaving for a quality-of-life gain nobody else would notice. The inconsistency would have stayed. So would, presumably, dozens of similar inconsistencies in every codebase I have ever touched.

Post-Claude, the same task took about thirty seconds of agent work and a quick review on my end. The gain is the same; the cost has fallen by two orders of magnitude. The threshold that determines what is worth doing has moved.

A counter-risk worth flagging, lest readers (and I) get carried away. When cosmetic cleanup costs this little, it becomes easy to spend agent time on tidying surfaces that did not actually need tidying. The discipline that survives the cost shift is asking, before each cleanup: would the inconsistency actually have bitten anyone? Sometimes yes. Sometimes no. The cost has fallen, but the threshold has not vanished. It has just moved to a place where more things make the cut.
